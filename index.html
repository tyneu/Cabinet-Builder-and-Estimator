    Board feet are the net 4/4 the parts need, multiplied by the allowance — lumber never yields perfectly. Only jobs with a run set to Boards pick this up; a job cut from sticks is priced per stick as before.
    Finishing sprayed
    Spray time comes from the fan width, overlap and gun speed. Dry time is tracked for scheduling but isn't charged as labour.

    How parts are figured

    • Inside parts = cabinet width − width deduction (15/16"), so they land in the 1/4" dados.
    • Uppers: 2 sides, full-depth top & bottom set in a dado 3/4" from each end, 2 stretchers, adjustable shelves.
    • The upper back is dadoed on all four edges, so it is the internal opening plus a dado depth all round.
    • The lower bottom runs the full cabinet depth, back panel or not.
    • Lowers: 2 sides on a separate toe-kick base, 1 bottom, and 3 stretchers — one at the top front, one at the top back, and a third on the back dropped below it.
    • A lower shelf is set back 1 1/4" when there's a back panel, or 1/4" when there isn't.
    • Length is always the longer dimension, width the shorter.
    • Widths round to 1/8"; leftover becomes even scribe gaps at both walls.
    • Every full sheet gets one breakdown crosscut between 36" and 60". A part longer than a half sheet (toe kick rails, long runners) is ripped off the whole sheet first, and the rest of that sheet is broken down after.
    • Sheet layouts come from the Plywood Cut Optimizer v2 search: identical parts are cut as batches from one parent blank with repeated fence settings, and the balanced layout is used.
    • Offcuts 6" or better on both sides and 1 sq ft or more go to inventory and get used on the next job.
    • Drawer boxes: sides run the full depth, front and back dadoed into each side, bottom in a dado all round. Stock thicknesses come from the Plywood & saw actuals.
    • Box depth takes the back-panel offset off the cabinet depth, then rounds down to the longest slide on hand. Overlay fronts on a face frame gain the frame thickness back.
    • On a face frame, the drawer opening is the cabinet width less one stile width, assuming the stile straddles the seam between neighbouring boxes.
    • Toe kick base: two rails on edge, a rung butted between them at both ends and every cabinet seam, plus one at mid-span on cabinets over 24", capped with two flat runners to screw into.
    • Face frame is one assembly per run: 2 end stiles, top and bottom rails, and a stile at every cabinet intersection.
    • Face frame and door frames are solid lumber, so they are listed as linear footage and never nested on sheets.
    • Shaker doors and drawer fronts: two stiles full height, two rails between them, panel captured in a dado all round.
    • Rails run the opening plus both dados; the panel runs the opening plus both dados less the clearance.
    • Frames come from 2 x 3/4 poplar in 8 ft sticks, with their own cut map. Anything narrower than 2" is ripped and the offcut is marked.
    • A face frame rail longer than one stick is glued from two pieces, jointed at a stile.
    • A cabinet wider than the two-door figure can be split into two openings with a centre stile.
